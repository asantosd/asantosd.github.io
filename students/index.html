JJ

Jesper Michiel Janssen

Principal advisor · In progress

Speeding Up Hardware-Aware Multi-Objective Search of Tiny Neural Architectures for Medical Image Super-Resolution

Designs very small deep neural networks that improve the resolution of medical images on low-power clinical hardware, where point-of-care computers often have under 100 MB of free memory. The project uses multi-objective evolutionary neural architecture search (HW-EMONAS) to balance image quality (PSNR/SSIM) against memory, FLOPs, latency and energy use. Surrogate models and zero-cost proxies cut the computational and carbon cost of the search. A planned protocol tests the diagnostic accuracy of the resulting models on mammograms.

AO

Alejandro Garma Oehmichen

Co-advisor · Completed 2026

Análisis basado en conectomas para neuropatologías no específicas en pacientes post Covid

Uses brain connectomics to study non-specific neurological and psychiatric sequelae in people who had COVID-19. With magnetic resonance neuroimaging, he compared structural brain networks of 100 subjects (51 post-COVID patients and 49 controls). The work focuses on the Mexican population, whose demographics, chronic comorbidities and genetic characteristics may shape how the disease affects the brain, and aims to add new knowledge about COVID-19's neurological consequences.

JQ

Jossian Abimelec García Quijano

Principal advisor · Completed 2024

Assessment of reconstruction accuracy for under-sampled 31P-MRS data using compressed sensing and a Low-Rank Hankel Matrix completion approach

Studies how to speed up phosphorus magnetic resonance spectroscopy (31P-MRS), which measures energy metabolites in muscle and brain but is rarely used clinically because acquisitions are long. Using compressed sensing with Low-Rank Hankel matrix completion, experiments showed that reconstruction accuracy depends heavily on which samples are kept rather than on the under-sampling factor alone, and that noise level has an even stronger effect. A journal manuscript was prepared from this work.

EC

Esteban Ricardo Salazar Cabrera

Principal advisor · Completed 2024

Named Entity Recognition in Mammography Radiology Reports using a Multilingual Transfer Learning Approach

Evaluates BERT-based language models for named entity recognition in Spanish mammography radiology reports, extracting findings such as BI-RADS category, symptoms and breast tissue descriptors. Several fine-tuning strategies were compared with precision, recall and F1-score; fine-tuning only the last two layers reduced overfitting and generalized best. Distinctive entities approached perfect scores, while rare ones were harder. Accepted at CBMS 2024, with a research stay at Universidad Politécnica de Madrid.

EP

Esteban Demián Elizondo Pereo

Principal advisor · Completed 2023

Assessment of Diffusion Weighted Imaging (DWI) Derived Metrics Variability for the Evaluation of White Matter Tissue Integrity

Addresses the lack of standardization in diffusion-weighted MRI acquisition and processing, which makes white matter results hard to compare across studies. Using coefficients of variation, he measured how much DWI-derived metrics, including fixel-based fiber density and fiber cross-section, vary with acquisition and processing choices. Goals: identify optimal acquisition parameters, build an optimized processing pipeline, and produce a healthy-subject template for reliable comparison across protocols.

EL

Eri Luna López

Principal advisor · Completed 2023

Clinical Characterization of Metabolic Syndrome Patients Using Clustering Techniques: A Study in the Mexican Population

Applies unsupervised machine learning to metabolic syndrome, a cluster of risk factors that raises the likelihood of cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes and is highly prevalent in Mexico. Several clustering algorithms are evaluated on datasets of Mexican patients to discover subgroups with distinct, clinically meaningful profiles. These subgroups are then characterized clinically, supporting more personalized risk assessment and treatment for the Mexican population.

DJ

Daniel Vela Jarquin

Principal advisor · Completed 2023

Multimodal medical image captioning with contrastive encodings

Builds a system that automatically writes clinically relevant reports from medical images. Instead of single-modality captioning models, he proposes an encoder-decoder deep learning architecture using contrastive learning (CLIP-style pretrained encoders, MedCLIP) with semantic and spatial attention, so one model can caption many imaging modalities. The system is compared against state-of-the-art modality-specific models and introduces multilingual caption learning with English and Spanish report databases.

SK

Sharif Nasser Kadamani

Co-advisor · Completed 2023

Analysis of Data-driven Diabetes Subgroups using Machine Learning for Prediction of Glycemic Control and Complications

Uses machine learning to support personalized management of type 2 diabetes. Building on data-driven diabetes subgroups, he trained classification models to find patterns within each subgroup and predict glycemic control, complications and treatment response from clinical characteristics. Done with the Metabolic Diseases Research Unit of the Instituto Nacional de Ciencias Médicas y Nutrición Salvador Zubirán using Mexican patient data, it aims to move diabetes care toward subgroup-tailored treatment.

BA

Berenice Jacqueline Sánchez Alvarado

Principal advisor · Completed 2022

Evaluation of white matter tissue integrity from COVID-19 recovered patients using diffusion weighted imaging

Investigates long-term neurological effects of COVID-19 (neurocovid) by looking for microstructural changes in the white matter of recovered patients. Using diffusion-weighted MRI, she analyzed tensor-based metrics such as apparent diffusion coefficient and fractional anisotropy, then applied fixel-based analysis and diffusion kurtosis imaging to handle crossing fibers. Statistical analyses found regional differences associated with intubation, comorbidities and disease severity, providing evidence of white matter alterations after COVID-19.
